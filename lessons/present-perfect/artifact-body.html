Present Perfect Simple

What Has Happened?

Level A225–30 minutes6 parts

Today you practise talking about things that happened in the past and are important now. Every Present Perfect sentence has a result you can see today.

I have lost my keys.

Past · actionI lost my keys. (When? Not important.)
→
Now · resultI can't open the door.

Present Perfect connects a past action with the situation now.

Part 1

Build the sentence

Write have or has in the first box and the past participle of the verb in the second box.

    Think: which words do we always need before the past participle?

    The form

    subject+have / has+past participle
    have
    I, you, we, they
    has
    he, she, it, one person (my wife, Edwin)

    Part 2

    What is the situation now?

    Match each result (1–5) with the correct sentence (A–E).

        Say it aloud

        For each correct pair, read the sentence and then answer the question “What is the result now?”

        “He has lost his glasses.” → “He cannot find them now.”

        Part 3

        Just, already or yet?

        Tap the best word for each sentence. You see the answer immediately.

        Quick rule

        JUST
        a very short time ago
        ALREADY
        sooner than expected / it is done
        YET
        until now; normally in questions and negatives, at the end of the sentence

          Part 4

          Detective

          Something has happened. Look at each situation and write what you think has happened. There are many good answers. Use has / have + past participle.

          Part 5

          Have you done it yet?

          Answer about your day. Don't answer only “Yes” or “No”. Give the short answer and one full sentence.

          Answer like this

          Yes, I have. I've already had breakfast.

          No, I haven't. I haven't had breakfast yet.

            After a Yes answer, you get one more question in the Past Simple (did…?). Answer it too: the action is finished, so now we use the past.

            Final challenge

            Five things in 60 seconds

            Tell me five things that have happened today. Press start and write (or say, then write) as fast as you can.

            1:00

              Targets

              Minimum
              5 correct Present Perfect sentences
              Stronger
              5 sentences with extra details (who? where? what?)

              Example: I've already had breakfast. I've checked my phone. I haven't gone out yet. I've spoken to my wife. I've learned some English today.
